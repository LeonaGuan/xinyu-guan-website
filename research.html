Research

My research examines K–12 school finance and resource equity, English learner and multilingual learner policy, and teacher labor markets, with attention to how policy design shapes whether resources reach historically underserved students.

Research Interests

I study how education funding and staffing policies are designed, implemented, and experienced across state contexts. My work pairs quasi-experimental methods using state administrative data with critical policy analysis to ask how public resources reach — or fail to reach — the students who need them most. Much of my current work focuses on how states fund services for English learners, how teaching capacity tracks student need, and what policy alternatives would distribute resources more equitably.

Dissertation

Aligning Resources with Need: Staffing, School Finance, and Bilingual-Bicultural Aid for English Learners in Wisconsin

Three-paper dissertation
Advisor: Christopher M. Saldaña

Expected Spring 2027

Wisconsin funds English learner education through Bilingual-Bicultural (BLBC) aid, a categorical program that reimburses part of districts’ approved costs rather than weighting students in the general formula. This dissertation asks whether that system turns English learner need into resources that reach students.

  1. Where are English learners in Wisconsin, and do schools have the staff to serve them? Links state staff records to enrollment data to test whether English learner teaching capacity tracks need.
  2. Funding that doesn’t follow: Structural versus responsive progressivity for English learners in Wisconsin school finance. Uses district finance panels to separate whether high-EL districts receive more from whether revenue responds when EL enrollment grows.
  3. Funding English learners with a single stream: A critical policy analysis of Wisconsin. Combines critical policy analysis with reconstructed BLBC payment records and simulations of alternative funding formulas.

The tool lets researchers, policymakers, and educators explore how states classify bilingual education — from mandated programs to restrictive policies — and compare legislative approaches and funding patterns across regions and over time.
