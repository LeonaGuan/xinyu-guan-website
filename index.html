Does school funding reach the students it is meant for?

Xinyu Guan

Ph.D. Candidate, Educational Leadership and Policy Analysis University of Wisconsin–Madison EdFund State School Finance Fellow, 2026–2027

I am a doctoral candidate in Educational Leadership and Policy Analysis (Policy Evaluation Track) at UW–Madison, advised by Dr. Christopher M. Saldaña, with a Ph.D. minor in Quantitative Research Methods. My research examines K–12 school finance and resource equity, English learner and multilingual learner policy, and teacher labor markets, using quasi-experimental methods with state administrative data. My dissertation, Aligning Resources with Need, asks whether Wisconsin’s school finance system turns English learner need into resources that reach students. I bring an interdisciplinary perspective informed by prior work in finance, auditing, and English language teaching across Canada, China, Thailand and the United States.

Research Interests

K–12 School Finance & Resource Equity

How states and districts raise and allocate money, and whether those resources reach the students with the greatest need.

English Learner & Multilingual Learner Policy

How funding formulas, eligibility rules, and program requirements shape services for multilingual learners.

Teacher Labor Markets & Staffing

Where teachers with the right preparation work, how staffing tracks student need, and what shapes who gets hired.

Quasi-Experimental Methods

Causal inference with state administrative data, including difference-in-differences, event studies, and regression discontinuity.
